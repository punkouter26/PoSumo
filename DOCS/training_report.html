The three charts that matter

These are the same numbers TensorBoard plots, redrawn here from its data so they could be annotated; they are not screenshots. One small chart per fighter, same scale across a row. Hover a chart for exact values. A dashed line marks where training was paused and restarted.

MattGrandmaNickKim

1. Skill rating (TensorBoard: Self-play/ELO)

What it means. Each fighter trains by wrestling saved older copies of itself. Beat the older copy and the rating goes up; lose and it goes down. A line that climbs means today's fighter is better than the one from a few minutes ago.

How to read it. Look at the direction, not the number. The rating restarts at 1200 every time training is resumed, so a drop at a dashed line is a restart, not the fighter getting worse.

Progress. Matt's first climb (to about 2050) is him learning to stand up at all. The other three started from Matt's brain, so they skipped that stage and their lines are flatter: they are improving in small steps from an already-standing start.

2. How long a bout lasts (TensorBoard: Referee/RoundSeconds)

What it means. The average length of a training bout in seconds. Training bouts are cut off at 20 seconds and called a draw.

How to read it. Near 1 second means the fighters are falling over at the start. Near 20 means nobody is winning: they stand until time runs out. Real sumo bouts last 5 to 30 seconds with a winner, so the healthy place is in the middle and coming down from the top.

Progress. Matt's line shows the whole story: one second at the start, then a jump to the limit once he could stand. Everyone is now near the top. After the lunge was added, bouts dipped at best (Matt's to about 7 and Grandma's to about 11 and Nick's to about 15 and Kim's to about 16 seconds), the first sign of fighters finishing bouts, but none has settled lower yet.

3. Points earned per episode (TensorBoard: Environment/Cumulative Reward)

What it means. Fighters collect points while training: a big one for winning, small ones for good habits such as staying upright, closing the distance and being chest to chest. This is the average total per episode.

How to read it. It is a health check, not a score. It rises when a fighter survives longer, because a longer bout has more time to collect small points. It can go up while the fighter is getting no better at winning, which is why the skill rating is the one to judge by.

Progress. Points rose sharply once each fighter could stay standing. A dip after "lunge added" is expected: a drawn bout now costs points instead of being free.

What is still unknown

Walking, getting up after a fall, edge defence and the belt grip are all in the training but have never been measured. The lunge and the dive are triggered by the referee, not chosen by the fighter. Fighters only ever train against copies of themselves, so nothing here says who beats whom; the only head-to-head results are a handful of bouts played in the game.
